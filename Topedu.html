Terms you will meet

Foundation year

A one-year preparation course before the degree. Some countries ask for it depending on your school diploma. If you accept it when it is mandatory, more universities stay open to you. Countries with direct entry skip it.

Entrance exams

Many medicine programs select students by exam, such as the IMAT for Italian public universities. Your advisor shares the dates and past exam models so you can prepare.

Recognition at home

If you plan to practise in Egypt, your degree needs Ministry and syndicate recognition. Tell us in the assessment and we shortlist universities with that in mind.

Costs and proof of funds

Plan for tuition, living costs and a financial guarantee for your visa. Public tracks in Germany and Italy are tuition-free or low-cost; Poland, Romania and Hungary have direct programs from about €2,000 to €5,000 a year. Italian regional grants can reach €5,400 a year.

Transfer credits, explained

If you already study at a university, you may be able to carry part of your finished courses to a new one, so you do not repeat them. This is called a credit transfer.

What is a credit?A unit of study workload. In Europe it is the ECTS: a full-time year is usually 60 credits, 30 per semester.
Who decides?The new university, course by course. It compares your syllabi with its own and accepts the ones that match.
When does it happen?After admission. You apply first, then the faculty reviews your documents. Expect partial recognition, not all.

Documents to prepare
  • Official transcript with every course and grade.
  • Course descriptions or syllabi for each course you want recognised.
  • Your university's grading scale and credit-hour explanation.
  • Proof of enrolment, with Ministry attestation and certified translations if the new university asks for them.
